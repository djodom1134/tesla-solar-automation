Raised only while surplus is actually being exported, and put back when the sun goes. Sitting near 100% is what ages the pack, not reaching it.

If you change the charge rate yourself

You and this controller both write the same setting, and without this the controller wins — move the slider in the Tesla app and the next loop writes its own rate straight back over yours. With it, your rate stands and solar control stands aside until you either stop and restart charging, or turn it back on from the car page. It only reacts to the charge rate — changing the charge limit, or stopping the charge, is not treated as taking over.

minutes

Below about 1.3 kW the car cannot charge at all. Grace holds at the minimum through short clouds instead of stopping — which imports a little. Set to 0 to never import.

Wh

The real limit on riding out a dip: how much grid energy the car may spend holding at the minimum before it gives up. An air-conditioner cycle runs 6–10 minutes and costs 120–200 Wh to bridge, so too small a budget pays most of the cost and still stops.

The gap between these two is the whole reason self-consumption pays. Credited the same as you are charged (full net metering) and using your own solar saves nothing — the grid is already your battery. Credited less, and every kWh you keep is worth the difference. There is no way to get a fully compliant audible warning through this device's API, so this only turns the garage light on and re-checks the door before closing — it never closes a door that is obstructed or that is no longer open by the time the wait is up. The ratgdo's own auto-close, configured in its own web UI rather than here, gives a fully warned close on an open-duration timer and is the safer option if you'd rather rely on that instead of this schedule.
